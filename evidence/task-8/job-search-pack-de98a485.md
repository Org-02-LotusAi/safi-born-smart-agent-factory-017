## Interview questions

1. **In your own words, what is a unit test, and how did you decide what to test in your booking API?**
   - Why they ask: Interviewers check that you understand testing basics, since the role says you will write automated tests for the code you ship.
   - A good answer: Explain a unit test in plain words, then give real examples from your capstone, such as how you tested the conflict-checking logic. Say only what you actually tested.
2. **Walk me through the Campus Study-Room Scheduler. What did you build, and what would you change now?**
   - Why they ask: A project walkthrough shows what you personally did and decided, and how honestly you describe academic work.
   - A good answer: Say it was a team capstone, name your part (booking API, conflict checking, unit tests), and name the tools (Python, Flask, SQLite, pytest, Git). Mention one real thing you would improve.
3. **Tell me about a time a bug or a disagreement on a team project slowed you down. What did you do?**
   - Why they ask: Teams want to see how you solve problems and work with others, and this role includes reviewing code with teammates.
   - A good answer: Pick a real moment from your capstone or a class project and use four parts: situation, your responsibility, what you did, what happened. Do not invent a story.
4. **This role uses Docker or cloud services, and I don't see them on your resume. How would you approach learning them?**
   - Why they ask: Interviewers want to see that you can be honest about a gap and show you are learning.
   - A good answer: Say plainly that you have not used them yet, name related work you have done (such as Python, Flask and SQL), and describe one small free step you are taking to learn. Do not claim experience you lack.
5. **What questions do you have for us about how the team does code review and supports new graduates?**
   - Why they ask: The questions you ask show what you care about, and this is a chance to learn about the role's day-to-day work.
   - A good answer: Ask one or two real questions tied to the posting, such as how new hires get code reviewed, how the team tests changes, or what the first month looks like.
